See a real Coach Aiden assessment of one of our runners ▼

"I've been watching your journey since December, and here's what the data tells me: You built something real in January and February. That 21-mile week in early March, the 10-miler at 10:32 pace with a 133 bpm heart rate — that showed legitimate aerobic efficiency. You proved you can handle structured volume when you commit to it.

But then life happened. The last three weeks averaged 3.6 miles per week. Your most recent long run was 4 miles. That fitness from February? It's not gone, but it's definitely not current. Your 4:00:00 goal translates to 9:09/mile for 26.2 miles. Based on your current fitness, if you could magically run a marathon tomorrow, you'd probably finish around 4:40–4:45. That's a 40–45 minute gap to close in 21 weeks.

Here's my three-tier honest assessment: I can practically guarantee we'll get you to 4:15–4:20. That's a legitimate marathon finish with smart training. We're shooting for 4:00:00 — that's our training target, and it's achievable if you execute consistently. And if everything clicks — perfect training block, ideal race day conditions, flawless execution — sub-4:00 is within reach.

But let's be clear about what has to happen: You need to rebuild that consistency you showed in January and February."

Real output from Coach Aiden. No filter. No fluff. This is what accountability looks like.

I wanted to get further in the run! But fizzled out a bit after that hilly climb. The realization that I just returned to Medellin from ~500 feet Texas a week ago — I am not fully acclimated to altitude yet! That's probably the reason.

Coach Aiden

Brad, that's exactly it. You're at what, 5,000+ feet in Medellin? Your body's still building red blood cells to handle that oxygen deficit — takes 2–3 weeks minimum to adapt. That fizzle wasn't fitness, it was physiology.

Here's what matters: you read it correctly and pivoted to the Cyclete instead of forcing more run miles. That's elite decision-making. The altitude stress is real training stimulus — your cardiovascular system is working overtime just to maintain easy pace.

Keep expectations realistic for the next week or two. Easy paces will be slower, HRs will drift higher, and that's normal. The Cyclete becomes even more valuable here — you can stack aerobic volume without compounding the altitude stress on your running legs.
