Earn It — how RunEQ training works

Aiden tells you the truth on day one. Earn It is how you close the gap.

No pace jumps because the calendar says so. You get faster when your training proves you're ready — which is how you get to your goal safely.

  1. An honest starting point

    Aiden reads your last six months of Strava. No quiz, no guessing. You get the real number — like 2:09 when you asked for 2:00.

  2. The plan bends

    Ride, hike, swim, or sandwich a run between two bike legs. Aiden coaches the workout you actually did, not the one on the calendar.

  3. Real insights after every workout

    A lap-by-lap read of what happened and what it means for your goal. Then ask him about it.

  4. Earn your level-ups

    Every week Aiden looks at all your running — easy days, long runs, hard sessions. When you've proven you're ready, faster paces are yours.

  5. Race-day confidence

    Checkpoints along the way prove the fitness is real, so race day is validation of work you've already done — not a gamble.

Other apps level you up off one fast workout. Aiden looks at your whole week.

Easy days kept easy, long runs finished under control, hard sessions executed — all of it counts as evidence. A single great interval session doesn't move your paces, and one bad day doesn't erase your progress.

After every run, Aiden reads what actually happened.

Not a map and a pace average. A lap-by-lap read of the session, in the context of your plan, your terrain — even your altitude.

“At 5,000 feet in Medellín, holding that effort is legitimately harder than sea level… which is exactly what that 155 bpm in Lap 4 reflects. You didn't blow up — you executed.”

Coach Aiden, reading a real tempo run

Then ask him about it.

Every workout read and every weekly review has a chat box. Why did my heart rate drift? Should I have pushed the last rep? What does this mean for Sunday? Aiden answers from your plan and your data — not a generic chatbot guess.

Before the run, you know exactly what today is for.

Every session comes with its structure, target and source — and Aiden's read on how to run it today, for you.

  1. The assignment

    Structure, pace, effort and where the workout comes from — Pfitzinger, Higdon, McMillan. The bar you see is exactly what your watch will show.

  2. Aiden's read on it

    Why this session matters for your goal, the mistake to avoid, and what to expect today — like your heart rate running high at altitude.

  3. Target vs. what happened

    Your run comes in from Garmin or Strava and lands next to the assignment: pace, effort, cost, terrain.

Every week ends with a real review.

Aiden looks at the whole week — what you ran, what you rode, what you skipped — and tells you straight. Completed 2 of 5 sessions? He'll say so, give you credit for what you did execute, and tell you what to put back on the calendar.

“The workouts you missed matter for development, and that's what we'll build toward. But what you did complete, you executed cleanly.”

Each review ends with what it means for your race, the plan for next week, and whether you've earned faster paces. And yes — you can ask him questions about that too.

Life happened? Tell Aiden.

Legs are trashed, the track is closed, work blew up. Tell him in plain words — or pick a direction — and he reshapes the day without losing what it was for.

Cross-training that's actually prescribed

Missing an interval day doesn't mean "go do 30 minutes of cardio." For that one session, Aiden offers 24 structured alternatives built to hit the same stimulus:

  • Pool running 400m repeats, 800m repeats, pyramids, speed bursts
  • Swimming 25s, 50s, 100s, descending ladder
  • Elliptical 1-on/1-off, pyramids, 3-minute repeats, Tabata
  • Rowing 500m and 1000m repeats, pyramids, max efforts
  • Bike sprints, 90-second power, 3-minute repeats, Tabata
  • Stairs power intervals, Tabata climb, pyramids, 30/30s

Cross-training earns RunEQ miles, so the week's work is counted honestly.

Questions runners ask first

Is Coach Aiden a real person?

No. Aiden is an AI coach. His workouts come from established coaching methods — Pete Pfitzinger, Hal Higdon, Greg McMillan and others — and he coaches from your actual plan and training data, not generic advice. He's direct by design: he'd rather tell you a goal is a stretch than promise something your training doesn't support.

Can I really ask him questions?

Yes. Every post-workout read and weekly review has a chat where you can ask Aiden about it — why a rep fell apart, whether to push Sunday's long run, what a heart-rate drift means. He answers with your plan and data in front of him.

What does Early Access cost?

Early Access is open for October at $15 per month, or $129 if you commit to a year up front. Payments go through Stripe. These are Early Access prices only — prices go up when RunEQ moves to General Availability.

Do I need to download an app?

No. RunEQ was built web-first: it works like an app on your phone with nothing to install, and opens full-size on your desktop for planning. Native Android and iPhone apps are coming soon, and your account will carry over.

Which watches and apps does it work with?

Garmin and Strava bring in your training history and completed runs. Structured running workouts can be sent straight to Garmin watches.

Do I have to cross-train?

No. You can run every workout as written. Cross-training is there when you need it, and it's prescribed with the same purpose as the run it replaces.

What are RunEQ miles?

A training-equivalent unit that gives credit for cross-training like cycling or pool running. They aren't literal running miles, and RunEQ doesn't claim every activity builds the same adaptations as running.

Does RunEQ diagnose injuries?

No. It can adjust training around what you report and support a gradual return to running after appropriate clearance, but it doesn't diagnose, treat, or replace a qualified clinician.
